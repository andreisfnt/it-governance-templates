## When to complete a DPIA

A DPIA is required under GDPR (Article 35) before starting any processing activity that is likely to result in a high risk to individuals. Mandatory triggers include:

- Systematic and extensive profiling or automated decision-making
- Large-scale processing of special category data (health, biometric, genetic, religious belief, political opinion, etc.)
- Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas

A DPIA is also recommended (and is good practice) for:

- New systems or processes involving personal data at scale
- Significant changes to existing data processing activities
- Introduction of new technology with privacy implications
- Data sharing arrangements with third parties

If in doubt, complete a DPIA. The cost of completing one unnecessarily is low. The cost of not completing one when required can be significant.

**Likelihood:** Remote (1) / Possible (2) / Probable (3)

**Common risks to consider:**

- Unauthorized access to personal data (breach)
- Accidental disclosure or loss
- Excessive data collection beyond purpose
- Retention beyond necessary period
- Data subject unable to exercise rights
- Inaccurate data causing harm to individuals
- Third-party processor mishandling data
- Cross-border transfer without adequate safeguards
- Re-identification of supposedly anonymized data
